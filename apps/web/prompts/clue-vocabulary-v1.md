## Clue vocabulary (v1)

A **Clue** is something visible in a Round that narrows down the countries. Each Clue is **one value in a family**: "Road lines: Yellow/orange centre line", never a loose "yellow lines". These are the only families. You may add a value inside a family when no listed value fits, but **never a new family**.

**Naming new values**
- Sentence case, British spelling, "<Value> <thing>", singular. Don't repeat the family in the name ("Wooden pole", not "Poles: wooden pole").
- Colours are one of eight groups: White, Black, Grey/silver, Yellow/orange, Red, Blue, Green, Brown. Close shades share a group (Norway's orange-tinted lines are "Yellow/orange"); keep the finer shade in the Claim's text, never in the Clue.
- Merged colours use "/" ("Yellow/orange"). Striped objects use "-and-" ("Red-and-white striped kerb").
- Chevrons are always "<background> chevron with <arrow> arrows".
- An absence ("No X") is a Clue only when a Claim treats the absence as a tell ("No front plate", "No outer lines"). There is no "No centre line": it usually just means a small road.
- Landscape Clues are things you can point at (red soil, rice paddies, eucalyptus, wind turbines, snow). General terrain, place recognition and coverage-extent facts get no Clue.
- A script or letter Clue names the letter and how it's said aloud: "Š (s with a hat)", "Ø (o with a slash)". Naming a language ("looks Czech") is a guess, not a Clue.
- Clues link to countries only, never to Regions.
